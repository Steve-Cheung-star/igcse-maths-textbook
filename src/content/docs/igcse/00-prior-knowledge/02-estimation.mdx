<section className="lesson-chunk">
Estimation is not about guessing; it is a strict mathematical process used to find an approximate answer. In IGCSE Maths, estimation questions test your ability to simplify an expression so that it can be calculated in your head. 

The golden rule that examiners look for is this: **You usually round every single number in the calculation to 1 significant figure (1 s.f.) *before* you do any maths.** If you calculate the exact answer on your calculator and then round it at the end, you will score zero marks!
</section>

---

<section className="lesson-chunk">
## 1. The Core Method: Round First, Calculate Second

To estimate an answer, follow a simple two-step process:
1. Round **each individual number** to 1 s.f. 
2. Perform the calculation using your new, simpler numbers.

<section className="lesson-chunk">
### Basic Operations (Add, Subtract, Multiply, Divide)

When dealing with fractions in estimation, treat the numerator (top) and denominator (bottom) as if they are inside invisible brackets. Simplify the top and bottom separately before dividing.
</section>

<section className="lesson-chunk">
**Worked Example 1: The Four Operations**  
By rounding each number to 1 significant figure, estimate the value of:
* **a)** $412.5 \div 18.9$
* **b)** $\dfrac{31.4 + 18.92}{4.81}$
* **c)** $\dfrac{8 920}{0.48}$

<details className="styled-details">
<summary>View step-by-step solutions</summary>
* **a) Estimate $412.5 \div 18.9$**
  * Round $412.5$ to 1 s.f. $\rightarrow 400$
  * Round $18.9$ to 1 s.f. $\rightarrow 20$
  * Calculate: $400 \div 20 = \mathbf{20}$

* **b) Estimate $\dfrac{31.4 + 18.92}{4.81}$**
  * Round top numbers to 1 s.f.: $30 + 20 = 50$
  * Round bottom number to 1 s.f.: $5$
  * Calculate: $50 \div 5 = \mathbf{10}$

* **c) Estimate $\dfrac{8 920}{0.48}$**
  * Round top to 1 s.f.: $9 000$
  * Round bottom to 1 s.f.: $0.5$
  * Calculate: $9 000 \div 0.5 = \mathbf{18 000}$ *(Remember: dividing by 0.5 is exactly the same as multiplying by 2!)*
</details>

<SteveTip title="The Decimal Division Trap">
Examiners love to include a denominator like `0.48` or `0.52`. When you round these to `0.5`, you will have to divide by a decimal. Do not panic! Dividing by a half (`0.5`) simply doubles your number. 
For example, $40 \div 0.5 = 80$.
</SteveTip>
</section>

<section className="lesson-chunk">
## 2. Advanced Estimations: Powers, Roots, and Brackets

When an expression includes indices (powers) or roots, standard BIDMAS/BEDMAS rules apply. You still round the numbers to 1 s.f. first, and then apply the power or root.

### The Square Root Exception
While the strict rule is to round numbers to 1 s.f., square roots and cube roots in estimation questions are carefully chosen by examiners. Sometimes, rounding to 1 s.f. gives you a number that is impossible to square root in your head (e.g., $\sqrt{61.4} \rightarrow \sqrt{60}$). 

In these cases, you round the number inside the root to the **nearest square number** or **nearest cube number** instead. 
</section>

<section className="lesson-chunk">
**Worked Example 2: Squares, Cubes, and Roots**  
Estimate the value of:
* **a)** $(8.24 + 2.91)^2$
* **b)** $3.9^3 \times \sqrt{98.5}$
* **c)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{38.9}}{0.21}$

<details className="styled-details">
<summary>View step-by-step solutions</summary>
* **a) Estimate $(8.24 + 2.91)^2$**
  * Round numbers inside the bracket to 1 s.f.: $(8 + 3)^2$
  * Simplify the bracket: $11^2$
  * Calculate the square: $\mathbf{121}$

* **b) Estimate $3.9^3 \times \sqrt{98.5}$**
  * Round $3.9$ to 1 s.f.: $4^3$
  * Round $98.5$ to the nearest easy square: $\sqrt{100}$
  * Simplify both parts: $64 \times 10$
  * Calculate: $\mathbf{640}$

* **c) Estimate $\dfrac{\sqrt{38.9}}{0.21}$**
  * Round $38.9$ to the nearest square number: $\sqrt{36}$ (which is 6)
  * Round $0.21$ to 1 s.f.: $0.2$
  * Calculate: $6 \div 0.2$. *(Hint: multiply top and bottom by 10 to get $60 \div 2$)*
  * Answer: $\mathbf{30}$
</details>
</section>

  </TabItem>

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="📝 Question 2: Fractions and Decimals">
    By rounding each number to 1 significant figure, estimate the value of:
    * **a)** $\dfrac{41.3 \times 19.8}{3.92}$
    * **b)** $\dfrac{68.5 - 11.2}{0.48}$
    * **c)** $\dfrac{9 125}{3.14 \times 2.89}$
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 2">
    * **a)** $\dfrac{40 \times 20}{4} = \dfrac{800}{4} = \mathbf{200}$
    * **b)** $\dfrac{70 - 10}{0.5} = \dfrac{60}{0.5} = \mathbf{120}$
    * **c)** $\dfrac{9 000}{3 \times 3} = \dfrac{9 000}{9} = \mathbf{1 000}$
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="📝 Question 3: Powers and Roots">
    Estimate the value of the following expressions:
    * **a)** $4.9^2 \times 3.1^3$
    * **b)** $\sqrt{82.4} \times \sqrt{26.1}$
    * **c)** $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{124}}{0.19}$
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 3">
    * **a)** $5^2 \times 3^3 = 25 \times 27$. *(For estimation, $25 \times 30$ or $30 \times 30$ is acceptable for a final step, yielding roughly $\mathbf{750}$ to $\mathbf{900}$. An exact $25 \times 27 = 675$ is also fine).*
    * **b)** $\sqrt{81} \times \sqrt{25} = 9 \times 5 = \mathbf{45}$
    * **c)** $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{125}}{0.2} = \dfrac{5}{0.2} = \mathbf{25}$ *(Since $5 \div 0.2 = 50 \div 2$)*
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="📝 Question 4: The Mixed Challenge">
    By rounding each number to 1 significant figure (or suitable squares/cubes), estimate the value of:
    $$ \dfrac{(18.2 + 21.5)^2}{\sqrt{15.8} \times 0.51} $$
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 4">
    * Round the bracket terms: $(20 + 20)^2 = 40^2 = 1 600$
    * Round the denominator: $\sqrt{16} \times 0.5 = 4 \times 0.5 = 2$
    * Put it all together: $\dfrac{1 600}{2} = \mathbf{800}$
  </TabItem>
