<Aside type="note" title="Hierarchy of Number Types">
## 1. The Number Zoo

Make sure you can identify these key types of numbers:

* **Natural numbers:** Whole numbers from 0 upwards (0, 1, 2, 3...).
* **Integers:** Positive and negative whole numbers, including zero (... -2, -1, 0, 1, 2 ...).
* **Rational numbers:** Any number that can be written as a simple fraction (like $\frac{1}{2}$, 0.75, or 5).
* **Irrational numbers:** Numbers that cannot be written as a simple fraction. Their decimals go on forever without repeating (like $\pi$ or $\sqrt{2}$).
</Aside>

### Special Numbers

Visualising these numbers makes them much easier to remember:

* **Prime numbers:** Numbers greater than 1 with exactly two factors: 1 and themselves (2, 3, 5, 7, 11...).
* **Square numbers:** The result of multiplying an integer by itself (1, 4, 9, 16, 25...). They can be arranged in a perfect square grid.

#### Method B: The Ladder Method (Repeated Division)

Divide the number by the smallest possible prime number. Write the answer underneath, and repeat until you reach 1.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
\mathbf{2} & 72 \\
\hline
\mathbf{2} & 36 \\
\hline
\mathbf{2} & 18 \\
\hline
\mathbf{3} & 9 \\
\hline
\mathbf{3} & 3 \\
\hline
  & 1
\end{array}
$$

<details className="styled-details">
<summary>View step-by-step division</summary>
<Steps>
  1. Divide 72 by the smallest prime (2): $72 \div 2 = 36$
  2. Divide 36 by 2: $36 \div 2 = 18$
  3. Divide 18 by 2: $18 \div 2 = 9$
  4. 9 doesn't divide by 2, so try the next prime (3): $9 \div 3 = 3$
  5. Divide 3 by 3 to get 1.
</Steps>
</details>

Using either method, you gather all the circled primes or the bolded numbers on the outside of your ladder. Write it out in index form (using powers):

$$
72 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2^3 \times 3^2
$$

### HCF and LCM from Standard Numbers

Let's find the **Highest Common Factor (HCF)** and **Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)** of two numbers: 24 and 60.

First, write out their prime factors:
* 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
* 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5

<Aside type="tip" title="HCF and LCM Rules">
* **HCF:** Multiply the prime factors they have in **common**.
* **LCM:** Multiply the HCF by all the **leftover** factors.

They share two 2s and one 3.

$$
\text{HCF} = 2 \times 2 \times 3 = 12

$$

## The Ladder Method for Two Numbers

When finding the Highest Common Factor (HCF) and Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of two numbers, doing separate prime factor trees can be time-consuming. The **repeated division** (or ladder) method allows you to process both numbers at the same time.

<Aside type="tip" title="The 'I' and the 'L'">
* **HCF (The 'I'):** Multiply only the numbers down the left-hand side (the common divisors).
* **LCM (The 'L'):** Multiply all the numbers down the left-hand side AND across the bottom row, forming an "L" shape.
</Aside>
</section>

## The Ladder Method for Three Numbers

Finding the HCF and LCM for three numbers follows the same ladder method, but introduces a crucial new rule when calculating the LCM. You must be careful to distinguish between when to stop for the HCF and when to keep going for the LCM.

<Aside type="caution" title="The Golden Rule for 3 Numbers">
* **For the HCF:** You must only divide by a prime number if it goes into **ALL THREE** numbers. Stop when no prime can divide all three.
* **For the LCM:** After finding the HCF, continue dividing if a prime number goes into **AT LEAST TWO** of the numbers. Bring down the number that cannot be divided as it is. Stop when all remaining numbers share no common factors (other than 1).
</Aside>
</section>

<section className="lesson-chunk">
### HCF and LCM from Index Form (Extended)

In IGCSE Extended, you are often given numbers already in index form. You do **not** need to expand them into massive numbers! Just compare their powers:

* **HCF:** Take the **lowest** power of each common prime factor.
* **LCM:** Take the **highest** power of *every* prime factor present.
</section>

<section className="lesson-chunk">
### Example 1: HCF & LCM in Index Form (Extracting Powers)
Given $A = 2^3 \times 5^2 \times 7$ and $B = 2 \times 7^3$, find the HCF and LCM of $A$ and $B$. Leave your answers in index form.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary><strong>View Solution</strong></summary>
  
  Use the power rules for index form:
  
  <Steps>
    1. **For the HCF:** Take the **lowest** power of the primes they have in common (which are 2 and 7. Note that 5 is not in both).
       * The lowest power of 2 is $2^1$ (from $B$).
       * The lowest power of 7 is $7^1$ (from $A$).
       $$ \text{HCF} = 2 \times 7 $$
    2. **For the LCM:** Take the **highest** power of *every* prime factor that appears in either number.
       * The highest power of 2 is $2^3$.
       * The highest power of 5 is $5^2$.
       * The highest power of 7 is $7^3$.
       $$ \text{LCM} = 2^3 \times 5^2 \times 7^3 $$
  </Steps>
</details>
</section>

<section className="lesson-chunk">
### Example 2: Finding Missing Exponents using HCF and LCM
Two numbers, $X$ and $Y$, are written as a product of their prime factors:

$$ X = 2^a \times 3^4 \times 5 $$
$$ Y = 2^3 \times 3^b \times 7 $$

The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of $X$ and $Y$ is $2^2 \times 3^2$.  
The Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of $X$ and $Y$ is $2^3 \times 3^4 \times 5 \times 7$.  

Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary><strong>View Solution</strong></summary>
  
  We use the rules for index form HCF and LCM backwards.
  
  <Steps>
    1. **Finding $a$:** Look at the prime factor $2$. $X$ has $2^a$ and $Y$ has $2^3$.
    2. The HCF uses the **lowest** power. The HCF has $2^2$. Since $Y$ has $2^3$, the $2^2$ must have come from $X$.
    3. Therefore, $a = 2$.
    4. **Finding $b$:** Look at the prime factor $3$. $X$ has $3^4$ and $Y$ has $3^b$.
    5. The HCF uses the **lowest** power. The HCF has $3^2$. Since $X$ has $3^4$, the $3^2$ must have come from $Y$.
    6. Therefore, $b = 2$.  
    7. <Aside type="note" title="Verification">
        We can verify this with the LCM: The highest power of 2 is $2^3$, and the highest power of 3 is $3^4$, which perfectly matches our given LCM!
       </Aside>
  </Steps>
</details>
</section>
