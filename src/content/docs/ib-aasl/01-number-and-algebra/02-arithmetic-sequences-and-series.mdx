<section className="lesson-chunk">
Mathematics is fundamentally the study of patterns. When a pattern consists of a list of numbers progressing by a constant addition or subtraction, we call it an **arithmetic sequence**. When we add those numbers together, it becomes an **arithmetic series**. 

In the AA SL course, you are expected to move beyond simply identifying the next number in a pattern. You will use rigorous algebra to find the $100^{\text{th}}$ term, sum the first $n$ terms using quadratic equations, decode $\Sigma$ (sigma) notation, and apply these concepts to model discrete real-world phenomena.

## 1. Arithmetic Sequences (The $n^{\text{th}}$ Term)

An arithmetic sequence is an ordered list of terms where the difference between any two consecutive terms is strictly constant. We call this the **common difference**, denoted by $d$. 

If the sequence is increasing, $d$ is positive. If the sequence is decreasing, $d$ is negative.

<Aside type="tip" title="1.2 The nth term of an arithmetic sequence">
  $$
  u_n=u_1+(n-1)d
  $$
</Aside>

Where:
* $u_n$ is the value of the $n^{\text{th}}$ term.
* $u_1$ is the first term.
* $n$ is the position of the term ($1^{\text{st}}, 2^{\text{nd}}, 3^{\text{rd}}$, etc.).
* $d$ is the common difference, found by calculating $u_n - u_{n-1}$ (e.g., $u_2 - u_1$).

### Example 1: Multi-Part Sequence Fundamentals

Consider the arithmetic sequence: $4, 11, 18, 25, \dots$

**a)** Find the next term in the sequence.  
**b)** Find the value of the $45^{\text{th}}$ term.  
**c)** Find the first term in the sequence that exceeds $300$.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Identify parameters for part (a):** 
       The common difference is $d = 11 - 4 = 7$. 
       The next term is $u_5 = 25 + 7 = 32$.
    2. **Calculate the arbitrary term for part (b):** 
       Use the formula $u_n = u_1 + (n-1)d$ with $n=45$, $u_1=4$, and $d=7$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       u_{45} &= 4 + (45-1)(7) \\
       u_{45} &= 4 + (44)(7) \\
       u_{45} &= 4 + 308 \\
       u_{45} &= 312
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    3. **Set up an inequality for part (c):** 
       We need to find $n$ where $u_n > 300$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       4 + (n-1)(7) &> 300 \\
       (n-1)(7) &> 296 \\
       n-1 &> 42.28\dots \\
       n &> 43.28\dots 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    4. **Interpret the result:** 
       Since $n$ must be an integer, the smallest valid value is $n=44$. To find the actual term, calculate $u_{44}$:
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       u_{44} &= 4 + (43)(7) \\
       u_{44} &= 4 + 301 \\
       u_{44} &= 305
       \end{aligned}
       $$
       The first term to exceed $300$ is $305$.
  </Steps>
</details>
</section>

### Example 2: Working with Arbitrary Terms

In an arithmetic sequence, the $5^{\text{th}}$ term is $19$ and the $12^{\text{th}}$ term is $47$.

**a)** Find the common difference, $d$, and the first term, $u_1$.  
**b)** Determine the general formula for the $n^{\text{th}}$ term.  
**c)** Is the number $105$ a term in this sequence? Justify your answer.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Set up simultaneous equations for part (a):**
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       u_5 &= u_1 + 4d = 19 \quad &\text{(Equation 1)} \\
       u_{12} &= u_1 + 11d = 47 \quad &\text{(Equation 2)}
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    2. **Solve for $d$:** 
       Subtract Equation 1 from Equation 2:
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       (u_1 + 11d) - (u_1 + 4d) &= 47 - 19 \\
       7d &= 28 \\
       d &= 4
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    3. **Solve for $u_1$:** 
       Substitute $d=4$ into Equation 1:
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       u_1 + 4(4) &= 19 \\
       u_1 + 16 &= 19 \\
       u_1 &= 3
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    4. **Find the general formula for part (b):** 
       Substitute $u_1$ and $d$ into the general formula $u_n$:
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       u_n &= 3 + (n-1)(4) \\
       u_n &= 3 + 4n - 4 \\
       u_n &= 4n - 1 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    5. **Test the value for part (c):** 
       Set $u_n = 105$ and solve for $n$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       4n - 1 &= 105 \\
       4n &= 106 \\
       n &= 26.5
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    6. **Conclude:** 
       Because $n$ must be a strictly positive integer ($n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$), $105$ cannot be a term in the sequence.
  </Steps>
</details>
</section>

### Example 3: Quadratic Relations and Multiple Common Differences

In an arithmetic sequence, the first term is $u_1 = 2$. The product of the second term ($u_2$) and the third term ($u_3$) is equal to $40$.

**a)** Show that the common difference, $d$, satisfies the equation $d^2 + 3d - 18 = 0$.  
**b)** Given that the sequence is strictly increasing, determine the value of $d$.  
**c)** Hence, find the value of the $20^{\text{th}}$ term, $u_{20}$.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Express $u_2$ and $u_3$ in terms of $d$ for part (a):**
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       u_2 &= 2 + d \\
       u_3 &= 2 + 2d
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    2. **Set up and simplify the product equation:**
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       u_2 \cdot u_3 &= 40 \\
       (2 + d)(2 + 2d) &= 40 \\
       4 + 4d + 2d + 2d^2 &= 40 \\
       2d^2 + 6d + 4 &= 40 \\
       2d^2 + 6d - 36 &= 0
       \end{aligned}
       $$
       Divide the entire equation by $2$:
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       d^2 + 3d - 18 &= 0
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    3. **Solve for $d$ and select the valid root for part (b):**
       Factorise the quadratic expression:
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       (d + 6)(d - 3) &= 0 \\
       d = -6 \quad &\text{or} \quad d = 3
       \end{aligned}
       $$
       Since the sequence is **strictly increasing**, the common difference must be positive ($d > 0$). Therefore:
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       d &= 3
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    4. **Calculate $u_{20}$ for part (c):**
       Substitute $u_1 = 2$, $d = 3$, and $n = 20$ into the formula $u_n = u_1 + (n-1)d$:
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       u_{20} &= 2 + (20-1)(3) \\
       u_{20} &= 2 + (19)(3) \\
       u_{20} &= 2 + 57 \\
       u_{20} &= 59
       \end{aligned}
       $$
  </Steps>
</details>

## 2. Arithmetic Series & The Story of Gauss

When we sum the terms of a sequence, we get a series. But how do we sum hundreds of terms efficiently? 

The famous story goes that in the late 18th century, a lazy school teacher told his class to add together all the integers from $1$ to $100$, hoping for a quiet hour. A 10-year-old Carl Friedrich Gauss walked up a few seconds later with the correct answer: $5050$. 

Gauss noticed an elegant pattern. If you pair the first and last numbers, the second and second-to-last numbers, they all sum to the same value ($1+100 = 101$, $2+99 = 101$, etc.). With 100 numbers, there are 50 pairs of 101. $50 \times 101 = 5050$. We can use this exact logic to prove the general formula for any arithmetic series.

<Aside type="tip" title="1.2 The sum of n terms of an arithmetic sequence">
  $$
  S_n=\frac{n}{2}(2u_1+(n-1)d) \quad \text{or} \quad S_n=\frac{n}{2}(u_1+u_n)
  $$
</Aside>

### Mathematical Proof of the General Formula

We can prove this by writing the series forwards and backwards, then adding them together:

$$ 
\begin{aligned}
S_n &= u_1 + (u_1 + d) + \dots + (u_n - d) + u_n \\
S_n &= u_n + (u_n - d) + \dots + (u_1 + d) + u_1 \\
\hline
2S_n &= (u_1 + u_n) + (u_1 + u_n) + \dots + (u_1 + u_n) + (u_1 + u_n)
\end{aligned}
$$

Because there are $n$ terms in the sequence, there are $n$ pairs of $(u_1 + u_n)$:

$$ 
\begin{aligned}
2S_n &= n(u_1 + u_n) \\
S_n &= \frac{n}{2}(u_1 + u_n)
\end{aligned}
$$

To derive the second version of the formula, substitute the $n^{\text{th}}$ term formula, $u_n = u_1 + (n-1)d$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_n &= \frac{n}{2} \Big( u_1 + \big(u_1 + (n-1)d\big) \Big) \\
S_n &= \frac{n}{2}(2u_1 + (n-1)d)
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 4: Finding the Generic Sum

Given the arithmetic sequence: $2.5, 6, 9.5, 13, \dots$

Find the sum of the first $35$ terms.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Identify the variables:** 
       $u_1 = 2.5$. 
       The common difference is $d = 6 - 2.5 = 3.5$. 
       We are looking for $S_{35}$, so $n = 35$.
    2. **Choose the correct formula:** 
       Since we don't know the last term, we use $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(2u_1 + (n-1)d)$.
    3. **Substitute and evaluate:**
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       S_{35} &= \frac{35}{2} \Big( 2(2.5) + (35-1)(3.5) \Big) \\
       S_{35} &= 17.5 \Big( 5 + (34)(3.5) \Big) \\
       S_{35} &= 17.5(5 + 119) \\
       S_{35} &= 17.5(124) \\
       S_{35} &= 2170
       \end{aligned}
       $$
  </Steps>
</details>

### Example 5: Algebraic General Formulas and Sums

The first three terms of a sequence are given by $8, 15, 22, \dots$

**a)** Write down an expression for $u_n$ in terms of $n$.  
**b)** Hence, show that the sum of the first $n$ terms can be written as $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(7n + 9)$.  
**c)** Find the number of terms required for the sum to strictly equal $855$.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Part a - General Formula:** 
       Identify $u_1 = 8$ and $d = 7$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       u_n &= 8 + (n-1)(7) \\
       u_n &= 8 + 7n - 7 \\
       u_n &= 7n + 1 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    2. **Part b - Prove Sum Formula:** 
       Use the $S_n$ formula involving the last term $u_n$: $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(u_1 + u_n)$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       S_n &= \frac{n}{2} \Big( 8 + (7n + 1) \Big) \\
       S_n &= \frac{n}{2}(7n + 9) 
       \end{aligned}
       $$ 
       *(This confirms the required proof).*
    3. **Part c - Solve for $n$:** 
       Set the sum equation to $855$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       \frac{n}{2}(7n + 9) &= 855 \\
       n(7n + 9) &= 1710 \\
       7n^2 + 9n - 1710 &= 0 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    4. **Solve the quadratic:** 
       Factorise the quadratic equation (or use the quadratic formula/GDC).
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       (n - 15)(7n + 114) &= 0 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
       This gives $n = 15$ or $n = -\frac{114}{7}$. Since $n$ must be a positive integer, $15$ terms are needed.
  </Steps>
</details>

## Sigma Notation ($\Sigma$)

When series become long and cumbersome to write out, mathematicians use a compact shorthand called **sigma notation**. The Greek capital letter sigma ($\Sigma$) translates directly to "the sum of". 

To fully understand and evaluate sigma notation, you need to know how to decode its four main components: the argument (the formula for the sequence), the index variable, the lower bound (starting point), and the upper bound (ending point).

### Example 6: Expanding Sigma Notation

Before jumping into formulas, it is crucial to understand what sigma notation is physically asking you to do. You can always evaluate a sigma expression by manually substituting the index values one by one and adding the results.

Expand and evaluate the following expression: $\sum_{k=1}^{4} (3k - 1)$

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Substitute the lower bound ($k=1$):**
       $$ u_1 = 3(1) - 1 = 2 $$
    2. **Increment the index by $1$ up to the upper bound ($k=4$):**
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       \text{For } k=2: \quad & u_2 = 3(2) - 1 = 5 \\
       \text{For } k=3: \quad & u_3 = 3(3) - 1 = 8 \\
       \text{For } k=4: \quad & u_4 = 3(4) - 1 = 11
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    3. **Sum the generated terms:**
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       \sum_{k=1}^{4} (3k - 1) &= 2 + 5 + 8 + 11 \\
       &= 26
       \end{aligned}
       $$
  </Steps>
</details>

<Aside type="tip" title="When to Expand">
  If the upper bound is small (e.g., $4$ or $5$), it is often faster to just list out the terms and add them up. If the upper bound is large (e.g., $50$) or unknown (e.g., $n$), you should expand the first 2 or 3 terms to find $u_1$ and $d$, and then use the arithmetic series formula $S_n$.
</Aside>

### Example 7: Sigma Notation and Inequalities

Given the series $\sum_{r=1}^{n} (5r - 2)$:
Find the least number of terms $n$ such that the sum of the series exceeds $1500$.

<details className="styled-details">
  <summary>View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>
  <Steps>
    1. **Identify the sequence parameters:** 
       Expand the first two terms to find the starting value and common difference.
       $$
       \begin{aligned}
       \text{When } r=1, \quad &u_1 = 5(1) - 2 = 3 \\
       \text{When } r=2, \quad &u_2 = 5(2) - 2 = 8 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
       This is an arithmetic sequence with $u_1 = 3$ and $d = 5$.
    2. **Express the sum in terms of $n$:**
       Use the formula $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(2u_1 + (n-1)d)$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       S_n &= \frac{n}{2} \Big( 2(3) + (n-1)5 \Big) \\
       S_n &= \frac{n}{2} \Big( 6 + 5n - 5 \Big) \\
       S_n &= \frac{n}{2}(5n + 1) 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    3. **Set up the inequality:** 
       We want $S_n > 1500$.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       \frac{n}{2}(5n + 1) &> 1500 \\
       n(5n + 1) &> 3000 \\
       5n^2 + n - 3000 &> 0 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
    4. **Find the critical values:** 
       Solve $5n^2 + n - 3000 = 0$ using the quadratic formula or a GDC.
       $$ 
       \begin{aligned}
       n &= \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4(5)(-3000)}}{2(5)} \\
       n &= \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{60001}}{10} \\
       n &\approx \frac{-1 \pm 244.95}{10} 
       \end{aligned}
       $$
       Taking the positive root gives $n \approx 24.395$.
    5. **Determine the least integer:** 
       Since $n$ must be a positive integer ($n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$) and the sum is strictly increasing (all terms are positive), the least number of terms needed to exceed $1500$ is $n = 25$.
  </Steps>
</details>

## TI-Nspire GDC Strategies for Sigma Notation

In Paper 2, your TI-Nspire Graphics Display Calculator is an incredibly powerful tool for bypassing heavy algebra when dealing with sigma notation. 

### 1. Fully Defined Sigma Notation (Direct Evaluation)
If you know the upper bound, you can just type it in and hit enter.
* **Accessing the template:** Press `math template button` (next to the catalog book) and select the $\Sigma$ template, OR go to `Menu > 4: Calculus > 3: Sum`.
* **Input:** Fill in the boxes exactly as they appear on the page (e.g., $\sum_{r=1}^{50} (5r - 2)$) and press `Enter`. 

### 2. Finding Unknown Bounds using Graphs and Tables
If you need to find an unknown upper bound (like in Example 2 above), the fastest method is often using a table of values.
* **Step 1:** Open a Graphs page.
* **Step 2:** Enter the sum as a function, replacing the unknown upper bound $n$ with $x$. For example: $f1(x) = \sum_{r=1}^{x} (5r - 2)$. 
* **Step 3:** Press `Ctrl + T` to open the Table view. 
* **Step 4:** Scroll down the $x$-column (which represents $n$) and look at the $f1(x)$ column (which represents the sum). Scroll until you see the first value that exceeds $1500$. You will see that at $x=24$, the sum is $1452$, and at $x=25$, the sum is $1525$. The answer is $25$.

<SteveTip title="The Danger of nSolve with Sigma Notation">
  Technically, you can use `nSolve` on a Calculator page to solve for a missing bound: `nSolve(Σ(5r-2, r, 1, n) = 1500, n)`. 
  
  **Warning:** This can be glitchy! `nSolve` relies on continuous calculus algorithms, but sequences are *discrete* (they only exist at integer values). The calculator might throw a "Domain Error" or run endlessly because $1500$ doesn't exist perfectly on an integer index. If you must use `nSolve`, replace $n$ with $x$, set the equation to equal the sum formula $S_n$ instead of using the $\Sigma$ template, and remember to interpret the decimal output correctly. However, the Graph/Table method is almost always safer and gives you a better visual understanding of the threshold.
</SteveTip>

    b) Find the value of the $35^{\text{th}}$ term.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 1">
    **Part a:**
    The first term is clearly stated:
    $$
    u_1 = 17
    $$
    Calculate $d$ by subtracting the first term from the second:
    $$
    d = 13 - 17 = -4
    $$
    
    **Part b:**
    Use the $n^{\text{th}}$ term formula with $n = 35$:
    $$
    u_n = u_1 + (n-1)d
    $$
    $$
    u_{35} = 17 + (35-1)(-4)
    $$
    $$
    u_{35} = 17 + (34)(-4)
    $$
    $$
    u_{35} = 17 - 136 = -119
    $$
  </TabItem>

    $$
    $$

    $$

    $$
    $$

    $$

    Consider the finite arithmetic sequence: $-5, -1, 3, 7, \dots, 103$.
    
    a) Find the number of terms in this sequence.
    
    b) Calculate the sum of the sequence.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 3">
    **Part a:**
    We know $u_1 = -5$, $d = 4$, and the $n^{\text{th}}$ term is $u_n = 103$. We must solve for $n$:
    $$
    103 = -5 + (n-1)(4)
    $$
    $$
    108 = 4(n-1)
    $$
    $$
    27 = n - 1
    $$
    $$
    n = 28
    $$
    There are 28 terms in the sequence.

    **Part b:**
    Since we know the first and last terms, we can use the shorter sum formula:
    $$
    S_n = \frac{n}{2}(u_1 + u_n)
    $$
    $$
    S_{28} = \frac{28}{2}(-5 + 103)
    $$
    $$
    S_{28} = 14(98) = 1372
    $$
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

    u_{15} = 3(15) - 2 = 43
    $$
    
    The number of terms $n$ is $15 - 1 + 1 = 15$.
    
    Now, use the sum formula $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(u_1 + u_n)$:
    $$
    S_{15} = \frac{15}{2}(1 + 43)
    $$
    $$
    S_{15} = 7.5 \times 44 = 330
    $$

    In an arithmetic sequence, the $4^{\text{th}}$ term is $21$ and the $10^{\text{th}}$ term is $51$.
    
    a) Find the first term ($u_1$) and the common difference ($d$).
    
    b) Find the value of the $20^{\text{th}}$ term.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 5">
    **Part a:**
    Set up two equations using the $n^{\text{th}}$ term formula:
    $$
    u_4 = u_1 + 3d = 21 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}
    $$
    $$
    u_{10} = u_1 + 9d = 51 \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}
    $$
    
    Subtract Equation 1 from Equation 2 to eliminate $u_1$:
    $$
    (u_1 + 9d) - (u_1 + 3d) = 51 - 21
    $$
    $$
    6d = 30
    $$
    $$
    d = 5
    $$
    
    Substitute $d = 5$ back into Equation 1:
    $$
    u_1 + 3(5) = 21
    $$
    $$
    u_1 + 15 = 21
    $$
    $$
    u_1 = 6
    $$
    
    **Part b:**
    Now find $u_{20}$:
    $$
    u_{20} = 6 + (20-1)(5)
    $$
    $$
    u_{20} = 6 + 19(5) = 6 + 95 = 101
    $$
  </TabItem>

  <TabItem label="📝 Question 6: Solving a Quadratic for 'n'">
    An arithmetic sequence is given by $8, 11, 14, 17, \dots$
    The sum of the first $n$ terms is $440$. Find the value of $n$.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 6">
    Identify the parameters: $u_1 = 8$, $d = 3$, and $S_n = 440$.
    Substitute these into the sum formula:
    $$
    440 = \frac{n}{2}(2(8) + (n-1)(3))
    $$
    
    Multiply both sides by 2 to clear the fraction:
    $$
    880 = n(16 + 3n - 3)
    $$
    $$
    880 = n(13 + 3n)
    $$
    
    Expand and rearrange into a standard quadratic equation ($ax^2 + bx + c = 0$):
    $$
    3n^2 + 13n - 880 = 0
    $$
    
    Solve using the quadratic formula or by factorising (or GDC if Paper 2). Factorising gives:
    $$
    (3n + 55)(n - 16) = 0
    $$
    
    So, $n = -\frac{55}{3}$ or $n = 16$.
    Since $n$ represents the number of terms, it must be a positive integer ($n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$). Therefore, we reject the negative fraction.
    
    Final Answer:
    $$
    n = 16
    $$
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

  <TabItem label="📝 Question 7: Contextual Application">
    A theatre has 25 rows of seats. There are $14$ seats in the first row, $16$ seats in the second row, $18$ seats in the third row, and so on, following an arithmetic sequence.
    
    a) Find the number of seats in the $25^{\text{th}}$ row.
    
    b) Calculate the total seating capacity of the theatre.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 7">

    This is an arithmetic sequence where $u_1 = 14$, $d = 2$, and we want to find $u_{25}$.
    $$
    u_{25} = 14 + (25-1)(2)
    $$
    $$
    u_{25} = 14 + 24(2)
    $$
    $$
    u_{25} = 14 + 48 = 62 \text{ seats}
    $$
    
    **Part b:**
    We need the sum of all 25 rows ($S_{25}$). We can use the formula with the first and last term:
    $$
    S_{25} = \frac{25}{2}(14 + 62)
    $$
    $$
    S_{25} = 12.5(76) = 950 \text{ total seats}
    $$
  </TabItem>

    The first three terms of an arithmetic sequence are $x$, $2x-5$, and $x+2$.
    
    a) Find the value of $x$.
    
    b) Hence, write down the first three terms of the sequence and state the common difference.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="✅ Solution 8">
    **Part a:**
    Because it is an arithmetic sequence, the difference between consecutive terms must be equal. Therefore:
    $$
    u_2 - u_1 = u_3 - u_2
    $$
    
    Substitute the algebraic expressions:
    $$
    (2x - 5) - x = (x + 2) - (2x - 5)
    $$
    
    Simplify both sides:
    $$
    x - 5 = -x + 7
    $$
    
    Solve for $x$:
    $$
    2x = 12
    $$
    $$
    x = 6
    $$
    
    **Part b:**
    Substitute $x = 6$ back into the expressions:
    * $u_1 = 6$
    * $u_2 = 2(6) - 5 = 12 - 5 = 7$
    * $u_3 = 6 + 2 = 8$
    
    The first three terms are $6, 7, 8$. The common difference is $d = 1$.
  </TabItem>
